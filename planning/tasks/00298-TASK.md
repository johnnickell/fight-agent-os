# Retire Feature settings safely after deployed reference removal

## Outcome

An authorized administrator can retire a rollout setting only after separately approved removal of its code
references has been deployed and complete current absence is established. A graduated capability can shed its
rollout flag without breaking retained running versions; later reintroduction starts with a new OFF identity,
not the old audience. One complete browser/server retirement path normally owns one PR, including its affected
lifecycle evidence rather than a separate reintroduction or proof-only TASK.

This is later lifecycle support. It neither schedules cleanup of `ai-template-proposals` nor blocks its initial
shipping or the first managed Team milestone. Its actual product owner decides when rollout is complete.

## Scope

- In scope: Existing management authorization/detail/confirmation and catalog invalidation; direct package
  `RemoveFeature` composition; trusted CURRENT-code/deployment safety qualification; exact reference release,
  uncertainty handling and reintroduction through existing protected provisioning; affected UI/contract evidence.
- Out of scope: Automatic cleanup TASKs, source removal during a request, deployment execution/approval, premature
  retirement of AI proposal gating, manual-create UI/API, Permission/grant deletion, force removal, new scanner,
  deployment platform, outbox, permanent name reservation, fake product Feature or separate lifecycle ceremony.

## Use cases and contracts

| Use case | Commands | Queries | Events | Expected side effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Establish retirement eligibility | Actual owner's separately approved cleanup/deploy, not a command supplied by this management API | Fresh trusted actual running-code identities, complete `FeatureReferenceDiscovery::discover(CURRENT)`, protected `GetFeatureById` | Owning cleanup/deployment facts only | Deployed reference removal or a safe hold; no implicit Feature deletion |
| Confirm and retire an unreferenced setting | Package `RemoveFeature` with stable `feature_id` and positive `expected_revision` | Current target and complete CURRENT discovery inside the package boundary | Package `FeatureRemoved` after commit | Full-state-checked removal and release of its Permission reference commit together |
| Reject or reconcile an uncertain removal | No automatic retry, force or name-based replacement | Fresh protected detail/catalog and trusted scope observation | No invented success fact; bounded local invalidation | Safe referenced/stale/unknown/unavailable/uncertain presentation; renewed human intent where appropriate |
| Reintroduce a name later | Existing separately authorized `ProvisionFeatures` | Fresh CANDIDATE discovery, current existing default Permission, then separate `ValidateFeaturePreparation` | New package `FeatureCreated` after commit | New identity, OFF, revision 1; no resurrection of settings or grants |

## Validation and permissions

### Existing authority and bounded intent

Consume TASK-00297's current `MANAGE_FEATURES` ADMIN_SAFE policy, initially assigned only to managed Super Admin,
and its protected detail/confirmation boundary. Require trusted actor, exact target and removal intent on every
supported HTTP/direct-bus/CLI/worker entry; unsupported modes remain unavailable, not new adapters for coverage.
Testing Permission, Role name, a stored ON/OFF value, UI visibility or bare `--internal` is not authority.
Privileged code/target/deployment observation requires its separately qualified operator scope; management rights
alone cannot select arbitrary code, credentials or another installation. Preserve current authentication,
same-origin/CSRF, bounded validation and safe correlated failures. Management remains reachable independently of
the retiring Feature's own availability; do not self-flag its confirmation or recovery controls.

Require an actual stable Feature ID and strict positive integer expected revision from current authorized detail.
Never infer a target by name or accept client-provided reference lists, scope labels or readiness Booleans as proof.
Bind confirmation to the current actor/target/revision and intended removal. Cancellation dispatches nothing;
stale state requires a new authorized read and confirmation, not silent retry against a newer revision or identity.

### Complete CURRENT absence and deployment-safety hold

This TASK remains `needs-info` until trusted knowledge of all applicable running versions/modules and protection
of the observation against conflicting activation are qualified for the supported target. Name the actual
operator/deployment owner, source/lock/module identities, participating versions/processes, supported overlap and
currentness limits. Unknown or unobserved coverage refuses retirement. Candidate absence, a local scan, a build,
TASK-00297's rollout readiness or TASK-00292's preparation result cannot establish this guarantee.

Reuse TASK-00290's single discovery/registration boundary. The actual product owner's separate cleanup must remove
all declarations, explicit registrations and runtime checks, including applicable browser and non-HTTP paths,
and deploy that removal before retirement. A retained native Attribute or registration rejects removal at every
status, including OFF and ON. Inaccessible modules, malformed/partial inventories, wrong scope, changed source,
bound exhaustion or scanner exceptions are not complete-empty results. An honestly complete empty CURRENT
inventory is valid; no demonstration Feature or forced cleanup is required to establish the reusable contract.

`RemoveFeatureHandler` requests CURRENT discovery within its canonical transaction, but does not fence another
version becoming active after inspection. Qualify the consumer deployment/activation policy's protection through
the removal decision and commit, and its treatment of retained/returning versions; neither a database reference
lock nor a prior negative scan excludes a later activation. Do not invent a package cross-deployment guarantee,
new readiness registry or universal deployer to fill the gap. An already-completed inventory may be supplied only
when its exact identity, freshness, completeness and protected observation are established. Bound discovery and
transaction duration; no nested transaction, arbitrary code loading, deployment or provider effects inside it.

Record a supported, accepted mechanism and attributable operational evidence before marking this TASK ready.
Unmapped deployment guarantees remain this explicit hold, not invented blocker IDs or a reverse dependency from
discovery/provisioning onto finished retirement. If a missing upstream contract is necessary, stop for separately
authorized owner work and qualification rather than patching vendor code or copying the handler. This is an
unqualified integration boundary, not a demonstrated exploit.

### Exact deletion and reference release

Dispatch package `RemoveFeature` through existing Common composition; consumer orchestration is limited to real
authorization, intent and deployment-safety policy under ADR 0001. Preserve the package handler's single canonical
transaction and events under ADR 0002. No forwarding alias, direct SQL bypass, hidden repository audit/policy,
outer transaction, copied scanner or second Feature store. Use the same qualified connection/reference fences
from TASK-00291 and preserve applicable lock ordering.

At the final `FeatureRepository::remove($expected)` boundary compare ID, immutable name, status, original
Permission ID and revision. A status/rebind change during discovery or before deletion must preserve the newer
record. Removal and Permission-reference release commit together and both restore on rollback. Permission removal
or managed reconciliation cannot commit against an uncommitted release that may roll back; remaining Role, Agent
or Feature references continue to block removal. Deleting the Feature does not delete its Permission or grants.
An otherwise safely removable broken binding needs no fabricated repair or Permission recreation first.

Qualify actual fresh reads, full-state races, constraints and reused ORM/connection behavior rather than relying
on the early revision check or existing storage tests alone. Discard closed/invalid transaction state after failure
through the owning invocation lifecycle, without unbounded retry.

### Uncertainty, browser state and reintroduction

The package returns `void` and publishes `FeatureRemoved` best-effort after commit. Post-commit publication failure
or lost acknowledgement does not establish rollback. Retrying an already-removed ID reports unknown; do not turn
that into proof that this caller's original operation succeeded. A later same-name record is a different target,
not a retry destination. Distinguish current observations from the uncertain original outcome and never infer
success from a callback/event alone. Sanitize command/error-bearing `CommandFailedEvent` handling and all scanner/
SQL/exception output. No new outcome ledger, outbox or eventual event-delivery promise is needed.

Use TASK-00296/00297's existing catalog/detail/private-data invalidation after success or uncertainty. Retire late
responses, stale confirmation and dependent data before bounded reauthorization/refetch; old successes/errors
cannot restore a retired identity or terminate a newer login. Reintroduction is decoded as a new ID, not patched
into an old cached row by name. Provide accessible confirmation, cancellation, referenced/held/conflict/unknown/
uncertain states and focus/retry behavior. No persisted business drafts, private HTTP caching, cross-tab authority
broadcast or automatic mutation replay.

Qualify later reintroduction by actual supported `ProvisionFeatures` followed by separate preparation and current
availability, not by adding a manual-create endpoint. The new identity starts OFF at revision 1 with the then-
configured existing default Permission. Missing required configuration fails atomically; repeated valid provisioning
preserves the new choices even if the default changes. The gap between deletion and provisioning remains unknown,
not ON or runtime auto-creation. Old delete/status/binding commands cannot mutate the replacement identity.
Prepared OFF is neither rollout approval nor action authority. No live reintroduction is authorized by this TASK's
planning approval; controlled lifecycle fixtures do not install a fake product capability.
