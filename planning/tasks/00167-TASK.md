# Enforce checkpoint retention and recovery holds

## Outcome

Protected checkpoint contents remain available while needed for active work, recovery or explicit holds, and
eligible terminal contents expire under the accepted retention policy. Users can distinguish retained audit
metadata from actual recoverable bytes without risking deletion of another session's context or source evidence.

## Scope

- Extend [TASK-00164](00164-TASK.md)'s protected checkpoint/companion lifecycle on Pi **1.1.0**, using
  [WF-030](../wayfinder/tickets/WF-030-define-context-retention-and-resume-contract.md)'s accepted WF-017 retention
  rules. Apply them to owned checkpoint and resume-only material, not arbitrary repository or user files.
- Preserve active, paused, recoverable and needs-human state. An idle process, closed terminal, disconnect or
  missing telemetry does not establish terminal completion. Use actual applicable lifecycle authority; local
  sessions require a supported explicit terminal disposition rather than fabricated managed Workflow state.
- After verified terminal completion, cancellation or failure, apply a configurable grace period initially **30 days**.
  Applicable evidence/recovery/security or other explicit holds prevent early deletion. Authorized extensions and
  early deletion use existing ownership/retention rules; configuring a shorter period does not create authority.
- Track attributable policy revisions, terminal disposition/time and hold/release facts bound to the proper
  session/checkpoint lineage. Recheck current authority, eligibility and holds immediately before deletion,
  including resumed work or a newly applied hold. Unknown state/time or unresolved race means preserve and report.
- Keep required predecessor state while checkpoint activation is incomplete. Do not remove a revision still needed
  by an active/paused/recoverable continuation or authorized descendant; resolve references before eligibility.
  Corrections and protected parent/child handoffs retain their own attribution without sharing private bodies.
- Cover owned native/companion checkpoint material through qualified owner-supported lifecycle operations. Keep
  Pi model-context ownership intact; do not destructively rewrite whole session logs merely to delete companion
  data or imply source files/audit history must be removed. Record exact retention coverage and any separately
  owned copies; unsupported required deletion semantics block that cleanup route instead of prompting blind removal.
- Enumerate only the operator-owned protected root and recognized owned records with canonical containment and
  bounded candidate/time/work limits. Never follow untrusted references or symlinks to delete arbitrary paths.
  Pin cleanup batch/reconciliation ceilings in the versioned profile before activation; interrupted deletion is
  recoverable and retries apply only to verified eligible owned artifacts.
- Preserve the existing separate categories for durable audit metadata and usage totals. Emit sanitized retention/
  hold/deletion facts and bounded availability metadata; metadata must never imply recoverable bytes after purge.
  Privacy/prohibited-sensitive-content rules still apply under holds; retention is not permission to keep secrets.
- On resume or historical inspection, report missing/purged/corrupt references explicitly. Do not recreate lost
  approvals or checkpoint facts from metadata alone or silently fall back to another role/session's private context.
- Exclude timing policy, optional Jev selection, source repair, whole-session transcript policy redesign, general
  audit-history cleanup, memory promotion, managed Workflow authority creation and any cleanup during planning.

## Use cases and contracts

| Use case | Commands | Queries | Events | Expected side effects |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Determine retention eligibility | Record an authorized terminal disposition or retention-policy revision | Current session state, references, holds and grace deadline | Disposition/policy fact | Protected lifecycle metadata; no deletion implied by idle state |
| Preserve needed recovery | Apply/release an authorized hold or extension | Current hold/recovery requirements | Hold/extension/release fact | Eligible retention changes under current authority |
| Remove eligible owned contents | Apply guarded retention cleanup | Revalidated scope/state/age/holds and owned artifacts | Deleted/skipped/failed outcome | Only authorized eligible checkpoint contents removed |
| Inspect or resume after purge | Existing resume validation; no reconstruction from metadata alone | Content availability and authorized audit facts | Explicit unavailable/blocked observation where applicable | Honest inspection or preserved pause; no invented recovery |

These operations follow current local/managed authority as applicable. This TASK does not introduce new grants,
an arbitrary file-deletion tool or cleanup of production databases.

## Validation and permissions

Consume TASK-00164's protected storage and TASK-00160's supported-route restrictions. Restrict policy changes,
terminal disposition, holds and deletion to their actual authorized actors. Verify session/branch ownership,
canonical paths and artifact identity; recheck eligibility on concurrent resume/hold changes. Same-user processes
and malicious trusted extensions remain outside the incremental guard's isolation guarantee. No audit/telemetry
record alone grants deletion permission. Respect existing privacy/disclosure restrictions and never send checkpoint
bodies to a model merely to decide deterministic age/hold eligibility. No Jev dependency is required for this policy.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Active, paused, recoverable and needs-human contents remain available, including disconnect/reload and
  incomplete checkpoint activation; process inactivity never starts a terminal grace clock by itself.
- [ ] Verified terminal contents obey the default 30-day configurable grace, authorized extensions/early deletion
  and holds; unknown time/state or new holds/resumption prevents premature removal.
- [ ] Bounded cleanup removes only eligible owned artifacts under current authority, with canonical containment,
  protected references and safe reconciliation after interruption. Source files and unrelated sessions remain intact.
- [ ] Native/companion retention coverage is qualified on Pi 1.1.0, with owner boundaries and unsupported routes
  explicit. No retained descendant/predecessor recovery reference is broken by an assumed terminal state.
- [ ] Missing, purged and corrupt contents are distinguishable from retained metadata; resume does not fabricate
  facts, approval continuity or successful recovery from an audit row alone.
- [ ] Attributable hold/policy/deletion observations follow existing privacy/history categories without raw context,
  credentials or invented managed identity. Hold authority cannot override prohibited-content rules.
