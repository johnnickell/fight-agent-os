# Strengthen architecture, testing, and two-pass review guidance

## Outcome

Project-local standards and skills give implementers and independent reviewers precise placement, CQRS/DDD ownership, proportionate behavior-testing, and separate Spec and Standards review checks. A bounded comparison identifies missing engineering skills, including audit and architecture review, without importing Factory workflows or silently adding new skills.

## Scope

- In scope: Compare relevant Fight Software Factory architecture, HTTP, naming, testing, work and review guidance with current Agent OS standards and skills; adopt only project-specific, compatible rules; update `docs/engineering/STANDARDS.md`, `docs/engineering/REVIEW.md`, and applicable `.pi/skills/work/` and `.pi/skills/review/` instructions; document a prioritized, scoped gap map for missing audit and architecture-review capabilities and other relevant engineering skills.
- Out of scope: Copying Factory skills, importing historical approvals or plans, implementing all missing skills, changing product behavior, introducing sub-agent infrastructure, imposing raw line coverage as a substitute for meaningful tests, or changing the existing implementation/publication/merge authority boundaries.

## Validation and permissions

Only the current project's decisions grant planning and engineering authority. A same-session builder's two internal passes are not independent approval; until sub-agent support exists, a separate reviewer invocation may perform two named passes without claiming two independent agents. Do not require tests of skills, Markdown text, configuration or quality tools in the product suite; verify guidance and planning with their owning tools. Existing security and server-side package/application policy remain authoritative regardless of transport guards.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] Standards specify responsibility-driven namespace/directory rules: only actual middleware in `Middleware`, transport-specific response mapping and representations in Adapter homes, Domain policy with its owner, and no speculative type buckets. Show how to evaluate existing exceptions without broad unrelated moves.
- [ ] CQRS/DDD guidance distinguishes package-owned use cases from genuinely owned Agent OS orchestration, guards against business policy rebuilt in handlers/adapters, and explains transport authentication/permission checks versus authoritative package/application target and ownership checks.
- [ ] Testing guidance demands meaningful success/rejection/failure evidence at the narrowest useful boundary and prevents test-only product routes, tooling-text tests, and unnecessary whole-app proof for a scoped change; bugs retain a failing regression where feasible. Keep the canonical gate and honest coverage evidence.
- [ ] `review` performs explicitly separate Spec and Standards passes with stable applicable criteria marked Pass/Fail/Unverified/N/A, evidence and limitations; all applicable criteria must pass to accept. A single independent reviewer can run both passes now without representing them as two agents or converting builder self-review to independence.
- [ ] `work` uses the new guidance as an internal completeness check while preserving separate independent review, branch/checkout choice, and no unsolicited publication or merge.
- [ ] A traceable, prioritized comparison of relevant Factory guidance and current local capabilities names what is adopted, adapted or rejected and why; it includes proposed separately bounded audit and architecture-review skills without implementing them prematurely.
- [ ] Demonstrate the Spec and Standards checklists directly on the TASK-00020 API/non-API boundary decision, explaining how the original globally applied JSend mistake would have been identified; do not add product tests of review tooling.
- [ ] Validate links, generated planning views, changed skill references, and `./bin/planning-check`; run `git diff --check` and `./bin/build`, reporting any warnings or verification limits.
